Composition and production · Harrisburg University

A name,
encoded in
sound.

The Harrisburg University Sonic Brand is an original composition that uses musical cryptography to connect an institutional sound with the university’s name and charter date.

Written and produced by A.J. Merlino

Final recording · 00:13

Used behind every Harrisburg University logo reveal for the online division and at live events.

Premise

A sonic identity with an internal logic.

Rather than treat the sound as a decorative tag, the composition makes the university’s identity audible. Its pitch material, opening gesture, and tempo each carry a specific point of reference.

The work is grounded in the E Mixolydian scale, whose seven tones make it possible to translate alphabetic characters into musical material. That cipher becomes the composition’s organizing device.

Method

A scale becomes a cipher.

Letters are arranged against the seven tones of E Mixolydian, allowing the name Harrisburg University to be expressed as a melodic sequence. The two low opening notes, E and D, establish the university’s initials before the name unfolds.

E F♯ G♯ A B C♯ DA B C D E F G
E F♯ G♯ A B C♯ DH I J K L M N
E F♯ G♯ A B C♯ DO P Q R S T U
E F♯ G♯ A B C♯ DV W X Y Z
Encoded sequence E–E–A–A–F♯–B–F♯–D–A–D–D–D–F♯–E–B–A–B–F♯–C♯–A

The melodic sequence spells “Harrisburg University.” The tempo of 120.2005 beats per minute commemorates the university’s charter date: January 20, 2005.

Context

Compositional history as a design resource.

Musical cryptograms have long allowed composers to carry names, messages, and private references inside a work’s structure. The B–A–C–H motif associated with J.S. Bach is one well-known example.

For this project, that tradition becomes a contemporary institutional composition: a concise piece of audio whose materials can be traced to the organization it represents.

The sonic brand treats history and identity as compositional constraints, making the final recording both an audible mark and a small, recoverable score.
